Shout out to my brother Joa for inspiring me to start a blog. I’ve read and enjoyed various blogs over the years, but I hadn’t previously thought of starting one myself. I think that’s because most of the blogs I’ve read have been sort of academic or polemical or trying to sell something or otherwise created for some other ulterior purpose. That’s not what this site’s about, at least not right now.

The specific idea from my brother’s blog that kind of stuck in my head and made the whole thing sound like fun is one that I now pose to you, dear reader: A blog is just social media, when you think about it. Which means you don’t need a domain of expertise wherein you’re the world’s leading authority, or a radical new theory the world must know about, or anything like that. All you need is something you want to say, and then to sit down and type it out. It’s conceptually not too different from the ‘gram or Facebook or whatever.

Well, it is a slower medium. Before you could read this post, I revised it, proofread it, and typed a few commands into Windows PowerShell to publish it to the Web. More on that later. The point is, this medium demands just a little more thought and effort than just thumbing out a Xeet from your workplace’s restroom stalls and smashing Post. What I’m Actually Doing Here

So I told you I liked the idea of writing a blog. Which to enough to sit down at the keyboard and get started, but a sentiment is not a reason or a purpose. It’s all good though. I think we all to some extent decide we like something (or don’t) and create our reasons later.

Years back, I had a conversation with one of my other brothers (Dominic) which turned out to be kind of revelatory to me. We were talking about phones. He told me that he’d recently bought a new iPhone and that the first thing he did after setting it up was, he looked up reviews of that exact phone in order to convince himself ex post facto that his decision to buy the phone was savvy and rational.

In the hour or so afterward, I realized that I do that sort of after-the-fact rationalizing all the time. And not just for my purchases of consumer goods, but also for my opinions, experiences, and so on. This is fine! In fact, I’m doing it right now and you, dear reader, are along for the ride. Writing as a Creative Outlet

Immediately after college, I felt like I’d kind of overdosed on reading and writing (I majored in English). Now my job title has the word “writer” in it, but I spend remarkably little time actually writing at work. I probably spend more time on graphics than on text, plus I usually have to collect, cross-reference, and collate a whole lot of raw information before typing anything into a document.

Looking back, some of that collegiate reading and writing was really cool and rewarding, especially the parts when my classmates and I read and reviewed each other’s essays/short stories/etc. It’s something worth doing more of, starting here. As for the genres of post that you’re likely to see, your guess is as good as mine the moment. Reviews, short fiction, poetry, updates on life and much more are all possible. In the future, I’ll probably settle on a narrower range of topics.

Establishing a Presence Online

I don’t use social media much. Not that I have a principled objection to it or anything. The stated aims of social media companies (bringing people together, providing an avenue for sharing experiences etc.) are really cool! Their actual aims (selling ad impressions and user data) compromise the experience in ways I dislike.

With a personal website, I can have the benefits of social media without having to look at a lot of dreck. If you’re someone who just met me and you Googled my name out of curiosity, there is now a useful measure of information about me that you can access at your convenience. Soon I’ll add contact info to this site, but for now you are welcome to reach out through whatever contact info you currently have.

Motivating Myself to Have Experiences Worth Writing About

Now that I’ve built a place to post stuff, I also have another reason to see and do things worth posting about. To do it for the content, as they say. Remember that bit about discerning the value of a decision after the fact? Conversely, now that I’m writing about the stuff I do, I have a prefabricated measure for the value of an experience, namely whether it would make for a good post. Therefore I have a whole new reason to do interesting things. Not that I really need another reason, but maybe it’ll lead somewhere new. We’ll see.

Creating a Sort of Mental Prosthesis

My range of attention is tiny. Estimates vary, but the general consensus is that the number of discrete “chunks” of information that a person’s working memory can hold is somewhere in the mid single digits, and that’s if the person is concentrating. I can only think about one thing at a time, most of the time.

Long-term human memory has drawbacks too. It’s generative rather than reproductive; every time I recall an event, my brain pieces it back together using current emotions, context, and so on. It’s sort of like a copying machine printing a recursive series of copies. You lose fidelity with each iteration. And that’s assuming I don’t just straight-up forget stuff.

Text doesn’t have these problems. Once I write something, it doesn’t change unless I go back and change it. So by blogging, I have a reliable record of anything interesting that I was doing or thinking about at the time of writing. There is no immediately obvious practical benefit to this, but it seems like the sort of thing that could have major non-concrete benefits somewhere down the line.

For this to work, I’ll try to post something every other week, without much regard for word count. One week might be really busy, but it’s unlikely that I’ll have two consecutive weeks in which I’m too busy to post something worthwhile.

A Note on Platforms and the Technical Aspects of Starting a Blog

To preface this part, I’m not a programmer. I created this site using Hugo, which is a static site generator. I chose this approach because a: I wanted to learn a little about how to build a website without needing to code everything from scratch, and b: I wanted to have the option to change the structure or look of the site in the future.

You don’t need to be a programmer to build a site this way! The software tools are all free and have excellent user documentation. However, that documentation assumes that the user already knows how to use their computer’s command line interface. And if you’re only a casual computer user like I am, then you’ll also need a certain patience and a willingness to learn by trial and error until it all starts to click. It took me a solid couple of evenings just to get a functioning site online.

If you’re not into that sort of tinkering and just want to start posting long-form stuff with minimum hassle, Substack is the best thing out there.
